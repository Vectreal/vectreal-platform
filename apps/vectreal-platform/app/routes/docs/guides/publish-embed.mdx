# Publishing & Embedding

Turn a saved scene into a publicly embeddable iframe.

---

## Publishing a scene

Publishing transitions a scene from **draft** to **published** status. Only published scenes can be accessed by external iframe embeds.

1. Open the scene in the Publisher or from the Dashboard scene detail page.
2. In the **Publish** sidebar section, click **Publish Scene** and confirm.
3. The scene status changes to `published` and a stable embed URL is generated.

To revert to draft, click **Revoke publication** and confirm.

Publishing compresses a copy of the model for visitors; the scene you edit is
left as it is. When the scene's lighting has a
[studio finish](/docs/guides/lighting#studio-finish) with **Depth** (or
`lighting.occlusion.mode: 'baked'` through the API), publishing also raytraces
ambient occlusion into each material's own occlusion map before compressing.
Any glTF viewer shows it, and it costs nothing to draw. Baking runs in your
browser and needs WebGL 2. When materials cannot be baked, a warning counts
them and names the first with its reason; the
[core reference](/docs/packages/core#occlusion-bake) lists the reasons. A
material whose model brought its own occlusion map keeps it and is not counted.
If depth fails as a whole, the scene still publishes, without it, and the
warning says so.

When the studio finish draws in the studio lights (**Studio lights** in its
settings, or `lighting.bake.lights: true` through the API), publishing also raytraces their light and soft shadows into a
light map for each material, after the ambient occlusion. Only the Vectreal
viewer draws it; other glTF viewers show the model without the studio lights.
Materials that cannot take it, and a bake that fails, are reported the same
way, and keep the lights live.

The baked maps live in the published file, so switching a bake on or off takes
effect on the next publish from the Publisher. Until then, embeds draw the file
they have with the new setting: switched to baked ambient occlusion, they show
none; switched away, the old baked map stays, under live occlusion if that is
on. A light map is drawn only while the lighting still matches what it was
baked from, so turning the lights bake off, or changing a light, brings the
live lights back at once.

> **Draft scenes are never accessible to external embed principals.** They remain viewable only by authenticated collaborators with project access.

### Show scene info

The **Publish** sidebar's **Viewer** section decides what the viewer draws besides the model. **Show scene info** adds an info button to the viewer, opening this scene's name and description. Switch it off for a scene whose title and copy already sit on the page around it.

A scene with nothing stored for it shows the button, so every scene published before the toggle existed renders exactly as it did. The setting belongs to the scene rather than to one embed: it applies to embeds and to preview links as soon as you save, with nothing to republish.

### Camera switcher

A scene with two or more saved cameras gets a switcher along the bottom of the viewer: arrows either side of the current camera's name and a count such as `2/3`. Past three cameras the name also opens a list of all of them. A hotspot's own camera never counts toward the two and is not one of the cameras the arrows step through; while a visitor stands at one, the switcher shows the hotspot's name, and either arrow leads back to the saved cameras.

The arrows step through the cameras in the order the **Camera** tool lists them, and the scene opens on the one at the top. Drag a camera by its handle to change the order, or focus the handle and use the arrow keys, Home and End. **Move to top** in a camera's own view makes it the opening view. A hotspot's camera is listed after the others and has no handle, since it is not in that order.

To leave it out, open the **Camera** tool and switch off **Camera switcher**. The toggle is offered once the scene has two cameras to switch between. Like **Show scene info**, it belongs to the scene and applies to embeds and preview links as soon as you save.

One embed can drop it without changing the scene: add `cameraControls=0` to the iframe `src`, as described under [URL parameters](/docs/guides/embed-sdk#url-parameters). Do that when your page switches cameras itself, so the visitor is not offered two controls for the same thing.

### Back to scene view

When a visitor clicks a hotspot that has its own camera, the view flies to it and the viewer shows a **Back to scene view** button. It, and Escape inside the viewer, lead back to the camera the visitor was on before, or the scene's default.

To leave it out, switch off **Back to scene view** in the **Publish** sidebar's **Viewer** section. The toggle is offered once a hotspot visitors can click has its own camera. Like the others, it belongs to the scene and applies to embeds and preview links as soon as you save. Switched off, a page around the embed can still lead visitors back with the SDK's [`returnToSceneView()`](/docs/guides/embed-sdk#methods), so hide it only where your page offers that way back itself.

### Show how to interact

New scenes show visitors a short line on how to move the scene when it first appears, such as "Drag to rotate, pinch to zoom" on a phone, worded for touch, a mouse or the keyboard and for the controls you left on. It goes on the first touch, scroll or key, or after a few seconds, and appears once per page view. The info popover lists the same under **How to move**. Switch off **Show how to interact** in the **Viewer** section to leave it out; scenes saved before it existed start with it off. An embedding page can withhold it with [`?hint=0`](/docs/guides/embed-sdk#url-parameters) or `setInteractionHint(false)`.

### Unload when off-screen

While a visitor scrolls the scene out of view, the viewer frees the graphics memory it holds, so a page with several scenes stays light on a phone. Scrolling back, it shows the last frame it drew until the scene is drawn again. Switch off **Unload when off-screen** in the **Viewer** section to keep the scene loaded instead: off-screen it only pauses, and it is back the moment it scrolls into view. That suits a page that scrolls back to the scene often. It is on for every scene unless switched off, applies to embeds and preview links as soon as you save, and is `presentation.unloadWhenOffscreen` through the API or the MCP server.

### Hotspots behind the model

Each hotspot decides what happens when the model sits between the visitor and the marker. Pick it under **Behind the model** when you select the hotspot in the **Hotspots** tool:

| Choice | What a visitor sees and can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Show** | The marker is drawn over the model from every side and is always clickable. |
| **Fade** | The marker fades while the model is in front of it, and takes no clicks or keyboard focus until the visitor turns the model to see it. An open card closes when its marker goes behind the model. This is what a new hotspot starts with, and what every hotspot saved before the choice existed does. |
| **Fade**, with **Clickable while faded** on | The marker fades the same way but stays clickable and reachable by keyboard, and its card stays open. It comes back to full strength under the pointer. Link a camera to it, so a click also brings the marker into view. |

**Fade** suits a marker the visitor should find by turning the model. A marker placed on the far side looks unreachable from the front with it, so if a hotspot has to work from anywhere, switch on **Clickable while faded** or use **Show**.

While you edit, markers behind the model stay unclickable whichever you pick, so a click on the model places a marker or turns the view rather than picking up one you cannot see. Switch to preview to try what a visitor gets.

### Hotspot cameras

Adding a hotspot in the Publisher also adds a camera of its own, framed from where you stood, and links the two. A click on the marker flies the view to that camera. That camera is the hotspot's close-up rather than a view of the scene: it is not one of the cameras the [camera switcher](#camera-switcher) steps through, and while the view stands there the viewer offers [Back to scene view](#back-to-scene-view).

**Linked camera** can also name one of the scene's own cameras, any you added in the **Camera** tool. A click then flies the view to that camera like the switcher would. It stays in the switcher, and no **Back to scene view** appears there, because the visitor is already standing in a view of the scene.

Visitors cannot step from one hotspot's camera to the next with the switcher. They click the next marker, and the way back still leads to the scene camera they started from.

### The Vectreal badge

Embeds carry a small **Vectreal** badge in the top right corner of the viewer on the free plan, linking back to vectreal.com. Paid plans do not.

It is independent of **Show scene info**: switching the info button off does not take the badge with it. Internal `/preview` links never render it at all, on any plan.

The badge is resolved from the plan of the organization that owns the scene, at the moment the embed document loads rather than when the scene was published. An upgrade therefore takes effect on the next embed load, not the next scene save. It follows the plan that is actually in effect, so a paid subscription that has lapsed is served as a free one and the badge returns.

## Preview API keys

External iframe embeds require a **preview API key** scoped to the project that owns the scene.

### Creating a key

The quickest route is the **Embed** section of a published scene: pick one of the project's keys, or click **Create a key** to mint one scoped to that project alone. Either way the snippet below fills itself in, and there is nothing left to substitute by hand.

To create a key with your own name, description or expiry instead:

1. Go to [Dashboard → API Keys](/dashboard/api-keys).
2. Click **New API Key**.
3. Select the project(s) the key should have access to.
4. Copy the key while it is shown. The list only keeps the last four characters.

A key created or rotated from now on is also recoverable: its value is stored encrypted alongside the hash, and the **Embed** section of a published scene reads it back to fill the snippet in.

Four kinds of key have nothing to give back:

- one created before that column existed
- a revoked key, because revoking clears the stored value on purpose
- an expired key, whose value survives but which the picker will not offer
- every key at once, if the server's `EMBED_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` is ever changed

Where a picker is on screen it greys those out with the reason. But the Embed section exists only on a **published** scene, and only for a project owner or admin, so it is not always somewhere you can look.

> **Do not rotate a key just to recover it.** Rotation issues a fresh secret and refuses the old one from that moment, so it breaks every embed still carrying the previous value. Rotate a live key you cannot read back; create a replacement for one that is revoked or expired, because rotation refuses both.

### Replacing a key you want out of circulation

A preview key appearing somewhere public is not, by itself, a breach: it is in the `src` of every embed already, so being readable is how it works. What restricts it is the owning project's allowed domain list - but that list is enforced by the browser sending a `Referer`, not by the server proving one, so it does not stop a determined holder of the key from reading the published scenes it points at. Replace a key when you want the old value refused, not merely because it was seen.

Refusal applies to everything an embed asks for from then on: the page and its scene. The files the scene points at (its model, its shadow and, when shown, its loading thumbnail) are fetched through signed URLs, handed out with the scene and cached by Cloudflare for every visitor. A signed URL carries no key, so it works for anyone who has it, from any site, until it expires at the end of the next UTC day, so between 24 and 48 hours after it was issued. Nothing you change reaches a URL already handed out: revoking or rotating a key or narrowing its allowed domains takes effect for those files only once their URLs expire, which can take up to 48 hours. The model is held that long even after **Revoke publication** or deleting the scene. The shadow and the loading thumbnail are checked again every few minutes, so a scene that is no longer published, or a thumbnail turned off, stops being served within about five minutes.

So the reason to replace one is that you want the old value refused - not that it was seen. **Rotate** rather than revoke: rotating issues a new secret for the same key, keeping its name, description, projects and expiry, so only the snippet has to change:

1. Go to [Dashboard → API Keys](/dashboard/api-keys).
2. Open the row menu on the key and choose **Rotate**.
3. Copy the new key. Rotation writes a fresh recoverable copy too, so the Embed picker can give it back if you lose it.
4. Paste it into every embed that carried the old one.

The old secret stops working the instant the new one is issued, so **every embed still carrying it is refused until you update it**. Plan the rotation for when you can update the embedding pages, not before.

The **Last Used** column is how you confirm the update landed: it is cleared by the rotation and stays empty until something authenticates with the new key. A key showing _Unused since rotating_ has an embed somewhere that is still sending the old value and being refused.

Rotation is offered only on an active key. A revoked key stays revoked, and an expired one cannot be brought back this way - create a new key instead.

Which is also the difference that decides between them: **rotating replaces the recoverable copy of the value, revoking destroys it.** A rotated key can still be read back from the Embed picker; a revoked one shows there as `(revoked)` and cannot build a snippet at all.

### Using the key

Pass the key as the `token` query parameter in the embed URL:

```
/embed/<projectId>/<sceneId>?token=vctrl_your_key_here
```

Or as a `Bearer` token in the `Authorization` header for API requests:

```http
Authorization: Bearer vctrl_your_key_here
```

> **Do not paste a quoted key into the snippet.** `?token="vctrl_..."` closes the `src` attribute at the first quote, so the browser requests an empty token and the embed answers `404` with nothing to indicate why. Copy the snippet from the **Embed** panel with the key already filled in, rather than editing the URL in place.

## Allowed embed domains

Every external embed is checked against the owning project's allowed domain list. The list is not an optional hardening step: a project with no entries rejects **all** third-party pages with `403`, even when the request carries a valid preview API key. Add the domain that will host the iframe before you ship it.

1. Open [Dashboard → Projects](/dashboard/projects) and select the project.
2. Click **Edit** and add entries to **Allowed embed domains**.

Supported domain patterns:

| Pattern         | Matches                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `example.com`   | Exact host only                 |
| `*.example.com` | All subdomains of `example.com` |

Requests from unlisted domains receive a `403` response, and the iframe says so in place of the scene: it names the host that was refused and points back to this section, so the host it names is the one to add. If it names no host, the embedding page sent no `Referer` or `Origin` (a `no-referrer` policy, or `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` on the iframe), and no entry on the list can match it. Let the page send its origin; the browser default, `strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, is enough.

One host is exempt: a page served from the Vectreal instance being embedded is always allowed, whatever the list says. That is why opening an embed URL from the dashboard - including the Embed panel's **Test embed URL** control, the arrow above the snippet - proves the key and the published scene, but never the domain list. Only a request from your own site tests that.

> **Local development** - there is no blanket localhost exemption. The automatic fallback only applies when the request carries no `Referer` and no `Origin` **and** the Vectreal instance being embedded is itself running on a localhost-like host. To embed `https://vectreal.com` from a local dev server, add `localhost` or `127.0.0.1` to the allowed domains.

## Embed snippet

After publishing, copy the embed snippet from the **Embed** section in the publish sidebar or from the scene detail page in the dashboard.

The hosted preview iframe can also be controlled from the parent page over `postMessage`. Use the standard snippet first, then add the advanced host-page control flow described below if you need synchronized camera changes, scroll-driven scenes, or temporary controls locking.

**Generated snippet** (what the Embed section copies). The box is `100%` wide and `400px` tall; both are plain inline styles, so edit them in the snippet after pasting it:

```html
<style>
	/* Lets the embed fill a bare flex parent, such as a Shopify Custom Liquid block. */
	@layer { div:not([class]):not([style]):has(> .vctrl-embed) { flex-grow: 1; } }
</style>
<div class="vctrl-embed" style="width: 100%; max-width: 100%; height: 400px;">
	<iframe
		src="https://vectreal.com/embed/<projectId>/<sceneId>?token=vctrl_your_key_here"
		style="width: 100%; height: 100%; border: 0;"
		allow="autoplay; xr-spatial-tracking"
		allowfullscreen
	></iframe>
</div>
```

> **Pasting onto a light page?** The viewer's chrome follows the visitor's own browser color scheme by default, which can leave dark controls on a light site. Add `&theme=light` (or `&theme=dark`) to the iframe `src` to state your page's scheme instead - see [URL parameters](/docs/guides/embed-sdk) in the Embed SDK guide.

### Why the snippet ships a stylesheet

`width: 100%` only resolves against a parent with a definite width, and the snippet does not own its parent. Where a host drops it into a flex row, that parent sizes to its content instead, and the only content-derived width an iframe has is the 300px every iframe defaults to. The embed then renders 300px wide inside a full-width section, and no edit to the snippet's own box changes it, because the element that needs fixing is the one above it.

A parent can only be reached from a stylesheet, which is why the rule is a `<style>` element rather than one more inline style.

Two things keep a rule aimed at an element the snippet does not own from doing harm. **`@layer` puts it in an anonymous cascade layer**, which loses to every unlayered declaration on the page whatever its specificity, so a theme that sizes this element keeps its own value and the rule applies only where the theme said nothing. **`:not([class]):not([style])`** then skips any wrapper the theme marked up directly. Both fail closed, on whatever the page already did: the theme's own width where it set one, 300px where it did not. Either way the failure is visible and documented, rather than a quiet resize of part of your page. So does a theme that puts another element between the flex item and the snippet, which defeats the child combinator. The one gap is a theme rule inside its own named layer, since the snippet's layer is declared later and wins.

Measured in a 900px-wide container, the embed's parent a bare `<div>`, and the sibling where there is one a fixed 100px block. Parent width, before and after:

| Parent's layout context             | Without the rule | With it           |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Flex row, embed alone               | 300px            | 900px             |
| Flex row, one 100px sibling         | 300px            | 800px             |
| Flex row, parent sized by the theme | 220px            | 220px, left alone |
| Flex column, height from content    | 900px            | 900px             |
| Grid, `1fr 1fr`                     | 450px            | 450px             |
| Block                               | 900px            | 900px             |

A flex column is where the rule is least useful, in both directions. `flex-grow` acts on the main axis, so in a column **with a definite height** it grows the parent's height rather than its width: in a 600px-tall shell the parent went from 400px to 500px beside that 100px sibling, or to 600px alone, leaving blank space under the embed. Horizon is in scope for this, because `layout-panel-flex` sets `height: 100%` and only the row direction resets it to `auto`, so a column-direction panel keeps that height at every viewport width.

And a column only escapes the width collapse through `align-items: stretch`, its default. Where a theme sets `flex-start` or `center` the parent is 300px wide exactly as in a row, and this rule does not fix it, because in a column the width is the cross axis. `align-self: stretch` is what fixes that one - see below.

`flex-grow` is the whole rule on purpose. An earlier version also set `min-width: 0`, which does nothing for this bug, because a growing item never reaches its automatic minimum. It is not free either. `min-width` is not initially `0` on a grid item, and that automatic minimum is what lets a `1fr` track widen to hold its content. Setting it to `0` gives that up: widen the wrapper to `700px` in a `1fr 1fr` grid and the track holds at 450px, so the box renders at the 450px its `max-width: 100%` clamps it to rather than the width you asked for. Nothing overflows and nothing is clipped - the size you set is just silently ignored.

To make the embed match the height of the row rather than its own box, change the wrapper's `height` to `100%`. That needs the parent stretched, which is the default; where a theme sets `align-items` to something else, add `align-self: stretch` to the rule you already pasted rather than to the element:

```css
@layer {
	div:not([class]):not([style]):has(> .vctrl-embed) {
		flex-grow: 1;
		align-self: stretch;
	}
}
```

Putting it on the element directly would mean giving that element a `class` or a `style`, which is what the selector skips, so the width fix would stop applying at the same time.

### Shopify: pasting into a Custom Liquid block

Horizon renders a Custom Liquid **block** into a `<div>` with no attributes at all. Where the section's direction is set to Horizontal, that `<div>` is a row flex item and the collapse applies; `column` is the default, and in that direction there is nothing to fix. Paste the generated snippet unmodified either way.

The Custom Liquid **section** is a different element. Horizon gives that one a class and an inline style of its own, so it is full width already and the rule correctly passes it over. Dawn wraps the same setting in ordinary block-level divs, where nothing collapses either. The block, in a horizontal section, is the case that needs the stylesheet.

Two things that do not work from inside that box:

- **`{{ section.id }}` is unavailable.** Shopify's [input settings reference](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/settings/input-settings) (accessed 2026-09-02) lists `section` among what a `liquid` setting cannot access, so you cannot scope CSS by section ID from there. That entry links to the `section` _tag_ rather than the object, so treat the exact boundary as unverified and test it before relying on it. The snippet's rule needs no ID, which is why it takes the form it does.
- **Unclosed tags are rewritten.** Shopify closes them when the setting is saved, and warns the result may not match your intended formatting - which is worth knowing for a snippet with nested elements. The field also caps at 50kb.

**Responsive 16:9 variant** (hand-written, if you want the box to scale with its column). Keep the `<style>` rule above if you paste this into a theme: in a flex row this box collapses to 0px rather than 300px, because an absolutely positioned iframe contributes no intrinsic width at all, so it disappears outright.

```html
<div class="vctrl-embed" style="position:relative;padding-top:56.25%;width:100%">
	<iframe
```

### Shopify: a section that tours the scene on scroll

[`vectreal-scene-tour.liquid`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vectreal/vectreal-platform/main/examples/shopify/vectreal-scene-tour.liquid) is a theme section that embeds a published scene into a Shopify product page, below the product details. The product gallery, Shopify's own 3D slot included, stays as it is, and the section needs no other code in the theme.

While the shopper scrolls through it, the scene stays pinned and the section calls `activateCamera` for each camera of the tour in turn. Orbiting is off during the tour (`setControlsEnabled(false)`), and controls that are off leave touch to the page, so a finger on the scene scrolls past it. Once the shopper stops scrolling for a second, a button in the scene offers to hand rotation over; scrolling on to the next camera takes it back. With reduced motion turned on, nothing moves on scroll: the scene shows its own camera switcher and the shopper turns it from the start. A poster shows until the scene is ready, and the scene starts loading only once the section is about to come into view.

1. In the Shopify admin, open **Online Store → Themes → Edit code**, add a section named `vectreal-scene-tour`, and paste the file's contents into it.
2. Add the shop's hosts to the scene's project under [Allowed embed domains](#allowed-embed-domains): its `.myshopify.com` address, which the theme editor previews on, and its own domain.
3. In the theme editor, open the product template, add **Vectreal 3D-Szene** below the product information, and fill in its settings.

| Setting | Takes |
| --- | --- |
| Embed-URL der Szene | The scene's embed URL with its `?token=`, as the Embed panel's **Test embed URL** opens it. Left empty, the section reads the product metafield `vectreal.scene_url`, so one product template serves every product |
| Kameras der Tour | Camera IDs in the order the tour visits them, separated by commas. Left empty, the tour visits every camera the scene reports, hotspot cameras included |
| Vorschaubild | The image shown until the scene is ready |
| Schaltfläche am Ende der Tour | The label of the hand-over button, `Selbst drehen` by default |

Each **Station** block adds one stop to the tour: a camera ID, a heading and a short text. With stations, the tour visits their cameras in block order and shows each one's heading and text over the scene, and the scene's own hotspot markers are turned off, so the captions replace them. A station whose camera the scene does not have is skipped, and stations take precedence over **Kameras der Tour**. With reduced motion, the caption follows whichever camera the shopper picks.

The section loads the [script-tag SDK](/docs/guides/embed-sdk) from unpkg at one pinned version with a Subresource Integrity hash, so a new SDK release reaches a shop only when its section file is updated.

## Advanced control

Hosted preview iframes support a small `postMessage` protocol layered on top of the standard preview routes.

### Message sources

| Source             | Direction            | Meaning                                               |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| `vectreal-host`    | Parent page → iframe | A trusted host page command or trigger update         |
| `vectreal-preview` | Iframe → parent page | A viewer lifecycle event or emitted interaction event |

### Host to preview messages

| Type                   | Payload                                                                                                                             | Effect                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ping`                 | none                                                                                                                                | Announces the host origin so the iframe can start delivering messages, and asks for a `pong`  |
| `viewer_command`       | `command: { type: 'activate_camera', cameraId: string }`                                                                            | Activates one persisted scene camera                                                          |
| `viewer_command`       | `command: { type: 'set_controls_enabled', enabled: boolean }`                                                                       | Temporarily enables or disables viewer orbit interaction                                      |
| `viewer_command`       | `command: { type: 'set_transition', transitionType: 'none' \| 'linear' \| 'object_avoidance', duration?: number, easing?: string }` | Overrides the transition used by subsequent camera switches                                   |
| `viewer_command`       | `command: { type: 'set_auto_rotate', enabled: boolean, speed?: number }`                                                            | Toggles and configures auto-rotation                                                          |
| `viewer_command`       | `command: { type: 'set_controls_options', zoom?: boolean, pan?: boolean }`                                                          | Enables or disables zoom and pan at runtime                                                   |
| `viewer_command` | `command: { type: 'set_interaction_hint', enabled: boolean }` | Shows or withholds the first-time hint on how to move the scene |
| `host_scroll_progress` | `progress: number`                                                                                                                  | Reports normalized host scroll progress used by persisted `host_scroll_progress` interactions |
| `host_message`         | `message: string`                                                                                                                   | Triggers persisted `host_message` interactions by message name                                |

Two field names differ between the SDK and the wire. `setTransition({ type })` sends
`transitionType`, because `type` is already taken by the command envelope.
`setZoomEnabled(enabled)` and `setPanEnabled(enabled)` both send `set_controls_options`,
each carrying only its own field (`zoom` or `pan`), so a partial payload is normal.

### Preview to host messages

| Type                | Payload                                                                  | Meaning                                                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pong`              | `sceneId`, `cameras`                                                     | Reply to `ping`; carries the scene's camera descriptors |
| `viewer_event`      | `event: { type: 'viewer_ready' }`                                        | The hosted preview is ready to receive viewer commands  |
| `viewer_event`      | `event: { type: 'initial_framing_completed', cameraId: string \| null }` | Initial framing and stabilization has completed         |
| `viewer_event`      | `event: { type: 'camera_changed', cameraId: string }`                    | The active scene camera changed                         |
| `interaction_event` | `eventName`, optional `payload`, optional `interactionId`                | A persisted `emit_custom_event` interaction fired       |

### Plain HTML example

```html
<div style="position:relative;padding-top:56.25%;width:100%">
	<iframe
		id="vectreal-preview"
		src="https://vectreal.com/embed/<projectId>/<sceneId>?token=vctrl_your_key_here"
		style="position:absolute;top:0;left:0;width:100%;height:100%;border:0"
		allow="autoplay; xr-spatial-tracking"
		loading="lazy"
	></iframe>
</div>

<script>
	const iframe = document.getElementById('vectreal-preview')
	const targetOrigin = 'https://vectreal.com'

	// The iframe cannot address the parent until the parent speaks first, so it
	// queues every outbound event (including `viewer_ready`) until then. Ping it
	// until `pong` arrives, otherwise a listen-only page receives nothing.
	let handshake
	const ping = () =>
		iframe.contentWindow?.postMessage(
			{ source: 'vectreal-host', type: 'ping' },
			targetOrigin
		)

	iframe.addEventListener('load', () => {
		ping()
		handshake = setInterval(ping, 250)
	})

	window.addEventListener('message', (event) => {
		if (event.origin !== targetOrigin) return
		if (event.data?.source !== 'vectreal-preview') return

		if (event.data.type === 'pong') clearInterval(handshake)

		console.log('Vectreal preview event', event.data)
	})

	function activateCamera(cameraId) {
		iframe.contentWindow?.postMessage(
			{
				source: 'vectreal-host',
				type: 'viewer_command',
				command: { type: 'activate_camera', cameraId }
			},
			targetOrigin
		)
	}

	function setControlsEnabled(enabled) {
		iframe.contentWindow?.postMessage(
			{
				source: 'vectreal-host',
				type: 'viewer_command',
				command: { type: 'set_controls_enabled', enabled }
			},
			targetOrigin
		)
	}

	function reportScrollProgress(progress) {
		iframe.contentWindow?.postMessage(
			{
				source: 'vectreal-host',
				type: 'host_scroll_progress',
				progress
			},
			targetOrigin
		)
	}
</script>
```

### Behavior notes

- This protocol is only available on hosted embed routes such as `/embed/:projectId/:sceneId`.
- Preview API keys and allowed embed domains still gate access before the iframe loads.
- A page that switches cameras with `activate_camera` should usually add `cameraControls=0` to the iframe `src`, so the viewer's own [camera switcher](#camera-switcher) does not compete with it.
- `set_controls_enabled` only affects runtime interactivity in the current viewer session. It does not rewrite persisted `controlsOptions` defaults.
- `host_scroll_progress` should be normalized to the `[0, 1]` range. The hosted preview bridge only re-fires range-based interactions when progress re-enters a configured range.
- `host_message` is intended for named host-driven triggers such as `next-step`, `open-details`, or `focus-product`.

## Embed route

| Route     | URL                          | Description                         |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Embed** | `/embed/:projectId/:sceneId` | Full viewport, immersive experience |

Supports the `token` query param and `Authorization: Bearer` authentication. A
request without either receives `404`, whether or not the caller is signed in.

Embeds published before this route existed used
`/preview/fullscreen/:projectId/:sceneId`. That URL still works: it issues a
permanent redirect to `/embed/...` and preserves the full query string, so
existing embeds keep their token and camera parameters.

## Status code reference

| Scenario                                            | Status                  |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Missing `projectId` or `sceneId`                    | `400 Bad Request`       |
| No `token` or `Authorization` header                | `404 Not Found`         |
| Legacy `/preview/fullscreen/...` URL                | `301 Moved Permanently` |
| Rate limited token validation                       | `429 Too Many Requests` |
| Disallowed embed domain                             | `403 Forbidden`         |
| Draft or non-existent scene (external access)       | `404 Not Found`         |
| Invalid, revoked, expired, or cross-project API key | `404 Not Found`         |
