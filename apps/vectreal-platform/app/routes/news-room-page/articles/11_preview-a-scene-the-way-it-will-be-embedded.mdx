You set up a scene across several tools. Cameras, shadows, the environment, hotspots, how the model responds to a drag. Preview mode takes the editor off the canvas and shows you what all of that adds up to, before the scene is published anywhere.

## Why it is worth a second look

Nothing about the scene changes in preview. Same renderer, same settings, same everything. What changes is that the scene gets the whole viewport, with nothing around it.

That turns out to matter for the decisions you have been making, because you made them in a canvas taking up part of a window, with a tool rail down one side and a panel open over it. Shadow darkness and ambient occlusion carry differently when the model fills the frame instead of two thirds of it. A camera transition timed against a small canvas can feel slow across a full page. Movement smoothness that felt controlled while you were dragging inside a panel layout can feel loose without one. A camera sequence you stepped through one at a time is a different experience when somebody walks it end to end.

That is the pass preview mode is for. Not a first look at something you just uploaded, but a look at the setup you actually built.

## What you are looking at is the embed

The page a preview opens is the page an embed renders. One component behind two routes, differing in how the request is authenticated and running the same saved scene either way. Cameras frame the same way, shadows fall the same way, a drag behaves the same way. Press `H` and the preview badge and camera switcher go too, leaving one faint restore button in the corner and, apart from it, near enough what a visitor gets.

So the check you do here is the check. If a camera clips into the model in preview, it clips on someone's product page, and there is nothing to confirm again after publishing.

Near enough, because two things are not identical, and both are worth knowing before you treat a preview as the last word.

An embed carries a small Vectreal mark in the corner on the free plan and loses it on the paid ones. Preview never carries it, on any plan, because it is our own page rather than a placement somebody bought. So a free-plan author is looking at slightly less than their visitor will be.

The color scheme is settled at each end by whoever sits closest to the page. Preview follows the app, because it is inside the app: the preview badge and the camera switcher are drawn in app tokens, and light chrome over a dark scene is its own kind of wrong. An embed sits on a page we cannot see, so it follows the visitor's own browser setting unless the host states one with `?theme=light` or `?theme=dark`. Worth looking at the scene both ways, or pinning the scheme in the URL and looking at the one you pinned.

## Two ways in

**From the canvas.** The **Preview Mode** block in **Camera Controls** clears the editing surface and leaves the scene in front of you, unsaved changes included. Save stays disabled until you leave. This is the one to reach for mid-adjustment, because it runs on the configuration you are still working on rather than the one on disk.

**On its own page.** **Open full preview**, in the same block, opens in a new tab and leaves the Publisher untouched behind it. The **Preview** button on the scene's page in the dashboard goes to the same place. Either way you are looking at the saved scene, so this is the one that answers whether what you committed is what you meant to commit.
