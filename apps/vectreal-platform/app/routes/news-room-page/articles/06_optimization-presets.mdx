The Publisher has three optimization presets: **Maximum quality**, **Balanced** and **Smallest**. Balanced is the default. The only thing that changes between them is how textures are treated. Everything they do to geometry is identical, and the biggest geometry saving, Draco compression, is applied when you publish rather than while you edit.

A note before the detail: I rewrote this article on 1 August 2026. The version that used to live at this URL described an earlier pipeline with four presets and a different texture stack, and the rework that shipped on 31 July made most of it wrong. What follows matches the Publisher as it is now.

## What a preset actually decides

Each preset is a texture policy. Textures are resized to a maximum dimension and re-encoded, and the preset picks the size and the quality:

| Preset | Max texture size | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum quality | 2048 | 90 |
| Balanced | 1024 | 80 |
| Smallest | 512 | 70 |

That is the whole difference. The pipeline's own guidance is that on a texture-heavy model this step tends to save more than every geometry technique put together, which is why it is the lever the presets expose.

![The Publisher's three optimization presets, each listing the techniques it runs, with Balanced selected.](/assets/images/newsroom/06/preset-cards.webp)

The preset cards list the techniques each one runs, and that list is read from the preset itself rather than written by hand, so a card cannot advertise a step the preset does not run.

## What every preset does the same

**Draco geometry compression is on in all three.** It is the biggest geometry saving in the pipeline, and it works by storing the same mesh at lower numeric precision rather than by rewriting it, so the shape survives. The compression is applied on the way out, when you publish. While you edit, the pass runs the encoder on a copy to measure what compression will achieve, shows you the result in the Geometry (Draco) row, and leaves the document you are working on at full precision. So the number you see after optimizing is a projection of the published output, and no amount of re-running the pass degrades your geometry.

One edge worth knowing: on small models, and on models where textures dominate, Draco can fail to make the file smaller. When the measuring pass has run and says so, publishing skips compression and the results row reports the step as skipped. The check comes from the pass, so a scene published without one never gets that comparison and is compressed regardless.

**Duplicate removal is on in all three**, because it is lossless.

**Vertex quantization is off in all three.** Draco already quantizes on its own, so running the separate pass on top spends time to lose precision twice. If you switch Draco off in the advanced panel, quantization switches back on so you are never left with neither.

**Mesh simplification is off in all three.** This is a change from the old presets, which discarded triangles by default. Simplification rewrites the mesh itself, and on the wrong model that shows up as gaps in surfaces, shading that breaks along seams, and stretched texture mapping. So it is now opt-in from the advanced panel, and the panel tells you what it does before you can enable it. Reach for it when the triangle count itself is the problem at runtime, not the download size. Draco handles the download.

## The advanced panel, when a preset is not enough

The presets cover the common cases. The advanced panel exposes each step individually:

- **Geometry compression** leads the panel. You can turn it off, or pick the method: Smallest (edgebreaker, best compression) or Sequential (less compression, keeps vertex order).
- **Texture optimization** gives you the full range the presets pick from: maximum size from 256 to 2048, a compression profile (Performance, Balanced, Max detail), and the output format, PNG, JPG or WEBP. The presets target WEBP.
- **Reduce polygon count**, the simplification step, sits at the bottom with its warning attached.

Settings apply to the whole scene. There is no per-material control, so if one hero texture needs more quality than the rest, the honest options are raising the whole scene's texture quality or preparing that texture at a higher resolution before upload.

## What a compressed scene costs at the other end

A Draco-compressed file needs a Draco-capable loader to open, and decoding costs the visitor something before the first frame. Scenes published and embedded through Vectreal decode in the platform's viewer, so there is nothing to configure. A Draco GLB downloaded from the publish sidebar and handed to another pipeline is a different matter: check the receiving loader first. I wrote about the underlying trade-off in [glTF vs GLB](https://vectreal.com/news-room/gltf-vs-glb-what-actually-matters?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=optimization-presets-and-texture-compression).

## Picking a preset in practice

Start on Balanced, which is where the Publisher starts you. Look at the scene at normal viewing distance. If textures visibly softened, step up to Maximum quality. If the scene is bound for mobile or sits in a grid of embeds, try Smallest and see whether you can tell. The pass never damages the document, so trying all three costs you nothing but the wait.

This is a pre-1.0 project. If a preset produces something that looks wrong rather than just smaller, that is a bug, and reporting it helps.
