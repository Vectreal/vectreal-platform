### Before You Begin

Camera configuration lives in the Vectreal Publisher's **Camera** tool, in the toolbar across the top of the editor next to Environment, Shadows, Interaction and Hotspots. How dragging the model feels lives one tool over, in **Interaction**. Open any scene in the Publisher and the controls described here are immediately available - no plugin, no extra package, no premium tier required. The Publisher itself is at [vectreal.com/publisher](https://vectreal.com/publisher).

If you have not published a scene yet, start with the [Publisher walkthrough](https://vectreal.com/news-room/the-vectreal-publisher-walkthrough). If you are integrating Vectreal into a React app, the camera configuration documented here is delivered through the same `cameraOptions` and `controlsOptions` props covered in the [React Viewer Integration guide](https://vectreal.com/news-room/react-viewer-integration).

### Why This Is the Lever That Matters

Three things determine whether a 3D embed feels professional:

1. The model.
2. The lighting.
3. The first thing the camera shows the visitor.

Most teams obsess over the first two. The third is what separates "interesting prototype" from "scene people actually use." A great model framed badly looks generic. A modest model framed well looks intentional.

This is the lever, and Vectreal gives you a full keyboard for it.

### What Ships Today - The Camera System in One Table

| Section | What it does | Where the visitor feels it |
|---|---|---|
| Cameras (Camera tool) | Save multiple named cameras per scene, pick the one the scene opens on, switch between them at runtime. | "Oh, there are presets - I can see different angles." |
| Field of view (per camera) | A 20°–120° slider with stops at Telephoto (35°), Standard (60°) and Wide (90°). | The whole composition. Wide vs. tight. |
| Transitions (Camera tool) | How switching to a camera animates: Instant, Linear, or Smart. | The moment between angles. Feels professional or jittery. |
| Interaction (its own tool) | Zoom on/off, auto-rotate on/off, feel, speeds, vertical limit. | Whether dragging the model feels good. |

These four pieces, working together, are the difference between "loaded a 3D file" and "shipped a presentation."

### Saved Cameras - The Concept Most Teams Skip

<video src="/media/images/newsroom/02/camera-preview.mp4" poster="/media/images/newsroom/02/camera-preview-poster.webp" autoPlay muted loop playsInline aria-label="A vintage camera with a hotspot on its lens. The Camera panel opens and the view moves in close on the lens for that camera, then preview mode clears the tool rails and panels away and leaves the camera alone on the page."></video>

The single largest quality jump comes from saving more than one camera. Most embeds ship with the default angle the file imported with - usually a corner-of-the-room shot that the modeler set up for working, not for showing.

In the Publisher:

1. Frame your scene in the viewport with the orbit controls until the composition reads.
2. Open the **Camera** tool and click **+** next to **Cameras**. The new camera takes the current view and arrives named `Camera 2`, `Camera 3` and so on.
3. Click it to open it, and change its **Name** to something the future-you will recognize: `Hero`, `Detail - Top Lid`, `Three-Quarter`.
4. Frame the next angle.
5. Repeat.

To re-aim a camera you already have, open it, frame the view, and click **Set camera to current view**. **Make default** pins the camera the scene opens on, and **Delete** removes one (a scene always keeps at least one camera). Cameras you created for hotspots show up in the same list, labeled with the hotspot's name.

Each saved camera stores its position, target, and field of view. Switching between cameras at runtime cuts (or transitions, depending on settings - more below) to the saved view.

**A useful default starting set is three cameras:**

- **Hero** - the angle the visitor lands on. Three-quarter front, slightly above the model's center of mass, framed so the silhouette reads cleanly against the background.
- **Detail** - close in on the part that matters most. The lid, the seam, the engraving, the texture.
- **Context** - pulled back, model centered, showing scale.

If you only had time to set up one extra camera, **set up Hero** and make it the default. The first frame is the entire experience for half your visitors.

Every camera has its own **Field of view** slider. It runs from 20° to 120° and snaps to three marked stops as you drag past them. This number is doing more work than most teams realize.

| Preset | Feel | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Telephoto (35°) | Tight. Compresses depth. Distortion-free at edges. | Product close-ups; detail shots; when you want the silhouette to read flat and graphic. |
| Standard (60°) | Natural. The default. | Hero shots; most product photography; architectural exteriors at distance. |
| Wide (90°) | Wide. Begins to distort straight lines at the edges. | Architectural interiors; immersive scenes; when you want the visitor to feel inside the model. |

A common mistake is leaving every camera on the same preset. **Vary it deliberately:** Standard for the hero, Telephoto for detail shots, Wide for context.

The stops are starting points, not the only options: drag anywhere in between, or double-click the slider to type an exact value. In code, the same per-camera `fov` goes through `cameraOptions` in [`@vctrl/viewer`](/docs/packages/viewer).

### Camera Transitions - Where "Polished" and "Jittery" Diverge

When the visitor (or your app code) switches from one camera to another, three behaviors are available. Pick one under **Transitions**, at the bottom of the Camera tool. The choice applies to the whole scene, not to one camera.

#### Instant

A cut. No animation. The camera teleports to the new position on the next frame, so the duration and easing settings go away when you pick it.

**Use it for:** kiosks, exhibition installations, anything where the visitor is using a remote and expects buttons to feel immediate. Also useful for accessibility - instant cuts are kinder to viewers sensitive to motion.

#### Linear

The default. The camera lerps from its current position to the saved camera over a chosen duration with a configurable easing curve. The **Duration** slider runs from 0 to 5000 ms with stops at Instant (0 ms), Quick (500 ms) and Slow (2000 ms).

**Easing options:**

| Easing | Feel | When to reach for it |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Mechanical, constant speed. | Rare - usually feels robotic. |
| Ease in | Slow start, fast end. | Subtle "pull" out of a static frame. |
| Ease out | Fast start, slow end. | Arrival into a hero shot - gives the destination weight. |
| Smooth (ease_in_out) | Slow start, slow end, fast middle. | Almost always the right answer for camera moves. |

Scenes default to **1000 ms** (one second) with Smooth easing, which is what a saved scene carries until you change it. For most product scenes that's the right number. Reach for Slow (2000 ms) on architectural interiors where the visitor needs more time to read the new space; Instant (0 ms) cuts with no animation at all.

#### Smart (object_avoidance)

A path-aware transition. The camera does not move in a straight line - it computes an arc that lifts over obstacles between the start and end positions. This is the transition that turns a model viewer into something that feels like a guided tour.

Smart is opt-in. When you pick it, a **Path settings** entry appears below the easing options with four parameters:

| Parameter | What it controls | Range | Sensible default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Obstacle Margin | Extra space the path keeps from any obstacle. | 0–20 | 2 |
| Path Height | How high the path arcs over obstacles. | 0–20 | 2 |
| Path Smoothness | Number of samples along the arc. Higher is smoother but more expensive. | 2–128 | 24 |
| Path Curve | How tightly the path follows the arc shape. | 0–1 | 0.5 |

Smart is the option to turn on when your scene has geometry between your cameras - interior architecture, a product with multiple components, a scene with a stage element. For a single object on a turntable, Linear is usually enough. The Publisher also warns that Smart framing is still unpredictable on some scenes, so watch the move in preview before you publish.

### Interaction - The Layer That Decides How "Good" Dragging Feels

How the model responds to dragging lives in its own tool, **Interaction**, next to Camera in the toolbar. Two toggles, one three-way choice, and a handful of sliders behind **Fine-tune** and **Advanced**, but each one shifts how the embed feels in the visitor's hands.

| Control | Where | Range | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zoom | Toggle | on/off | Whether scroll-zoom is allowed. Off for kiosks. On for everything else. |
| Auto rotate | Toggle | on/off | Whether the camera spins idly when the visitor is not interacting. Useful for marketing pages where the embed is in the visitor's peripheral vision. |
| Feel | Floaty / Balanced / Snappy | **Default Balanced** | How closely the scene follows a drag. Floaty lags behind the pointer; Snappy tracks it tightly. Picking one sets Responsiveness and the orbit, pan and zoom speeds at once. |
| Responsiveness | Fine-tune | 0.02–1 | How closely the camera tracks the pointer. |
| Orbit Speed | Fine-tune | 0.1–3 | Drag-rotation speed. |
| Pan Speed | Fine-tune | 0.1–3 | Right-click pan speed. |
| Zoom Speed | Fine-tune | 0.1–5 | Scroll-zoom step size. Disabled while Zoom is off. |
| Rotation Speed | Fine-tune | 0.1–10 | Auto-rotate speed. **Default 0.25.** Above 3 starts to feel showroom-spinny. Disabled while Auto rotate is off. |
| Vertical Limit | Advanced | 0–180° | How far down the camera can orbit. 90° keeps the camera above the horizon - saves visitors from the inside-out view of a model with no underside. |

**The single most-impactful one** for most embeds is **Vertical Limit**. New scenes start at **90°**; leave it there unless the underside is worth seeing, and check it on any scene where visitors keep ending up under the floor.

### Three Preset Bundles That Match Real Use Cases

You can think of all of the above as a kit, and reach into it differently depending on the scene's job. Three starting points that work well in production:

#### E-commerce product (single object on a "stage")

**Cameras:** Hero (3/4 front, Standard 60°), Detail (closeup of the most-photographed feature, Telephoto 35°), Context (pulled back, Wide 90°).
**Transitions:** Linear, Smooth easing, Quick (500 ms).
**Interaction:** Zoom on. Auto-rotate **off** (it competes with intentional product photography). Vertical Limit 90°. Feel: Balanced.

The visitor lands on Hero, can drag to inspect, can switch to Detail to see the feature that closes the sale.

#### Architectural interior

**Cameras:** Entry (Wide 90°), Living Area (Standard 60°), Detail (Telephoto 35° on a finish or fixture).
**Transitions:** Smart, with Path Height 3, Obstacle Margin 2, Path Smoothness 32. Duration Slow (2000 ms) - interiors need time to read.
**Interaction:** Zoom on. Auto-rotate off. Vertical Limit 100° to allow looking slightly down at floor finishes.

Smart transitions are doing real work here - without them, switching between cameras inside a building cuts straight through walls.

#### Guided product demo (presentation mode)

**Cameras:** an ordered tour - `01 - Open`, `02 - Top`, `03 - Inside`, `04 - Detail`, `05 - Closeout`. Each numbered for predictable iteration.
**Transitions:** Linear or Smart depending on geometry. Smooth easing. **Duration Slow (2000 ms)** to give each step room to breathe.
**Interaction:** Zoom off. Auto-rotate off. The visitor's job is to **press Next**, not to free-fly.

This is the layout that turns a 3D embed into a slide deck. Combined with a few buttons in your surrounding UI that switch cameras through the [embed SDK](/docs/guides/embed-sdk), it becomes a presentation people watch start to finish.

### A Five-Minute Polish Pass

If you have shipped a Vectreal scene and want to upgrade it before doing anything else:

1. Open the scene in the Publisher.
2. Frame a Hero view. In the Camera tool, add it as a camera, name it `Hero`, set Field of view to Standard (60°), and click **Make default**.
3. In Interaction, open **Advanced** and check Vertical Limit sits at 90°.
4. Under **Transitions**, pick Linear, Smooth easing, Quick (500 ms).
5. Re-publish.

That's it. Five minutes. The first frame is now intentional, the visitor cannot fall through the floor, and any future camera switch animates instead of teleporting. The visible quality jump is significant.
