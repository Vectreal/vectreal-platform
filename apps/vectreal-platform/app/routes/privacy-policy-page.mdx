## 2. Data We Collect

We collect the following categories of data, depending on how you use the platform:

- **Account and profile data:** name, email address, and account identifiers.
- **Onboarding data:** optional information you provide during onboarding, such as your role, use case, company name, and how you heard about Vectreal.
- **Authentication data:** session and identity data processed through Supabase Auth, including sign-in with email and password or optional Google or GitHub single sign-on.
- **Billing and subscription data:** plan, billing status, and payment-related identifiers (such as Stripe customer and subscription IDs). Card payments are processed by Stripe; we do not store full card numbers.
- **Workspace and content data:** organizations and memberships, projects, scenes, uploaded 3D models and related assets, optimization metadata, thumbnails, and related logs. Uploaded assets are stored in private storage and served through our application with permission checks.
- **API keys:** keys you generate to access or embed content are stored in hashed form together with a short non-sensitive preview.
- **Contact and support data:** information you submit through the contact form, including your name, email address, inquiry type, and message. Your name, email address, and message are encrypted at rest; the inquiry type is stored in plain text.
- **Free-model requests:** if you request a free 3D model at /free-model, we process your name, work email, company, product type, the optional size and message, the three photos you upload, and the letter or letter batch the link came from. We use them to make and deliver the model and, if you agreed, to email you about it and about a paid pilot. Name, email, company and message are encrypted at rest. Photos are re-encoded on upload, which removes their metadata (including any location), and kept in a private storage bucket that is never publicly readable. You can withdraw your consent to follow-up emails at any time.
- **Letter addresses:** when you open the short address printed on one of our letters (vectreal.com/c/ followed by a code), we record that this letter's code was opened and when. We record nothing else about the visit, such as your IP address or device.
- **Visit counts without consent:** if you have not accepted analytics, we count page views and a few steps from our newsroom to sign-up without cookies, as described in Section 4 under "Counting Without Consent".
- **Consent and preference data:** your consent choices for functional, analytics, and marketing categories, the consent policy version, a country derived from your IP address, and your browser user agent. This record is stored only if you have an account. If you are not signed in, your consent choices stay in the `consent_prefs` cookie on your device and no server-side record is created.
- **Technical and security data:** IP address and request metadata used transiently for security, rate limiting, and bot protection, and session and CSRF tokens required to operate the service. We do not persist your full IP address for consent records; we store only the derived country.

We do not intentionally collect special categories of data. Please do not submit sensitive personal data in free-text fields.

## 4. Cookies, Consent, and Tracking

Vectreal uses cookies and similar storage mechanisms for core operation and, with your consent, for optional features. Full product analytics (PostHog) runs only after you opt in; until then, PostHog only counts visits without cookies, as described below.

### Consent Categories

- **Strictly necessary:** always enabled; required for authentication, CSRF protection, storing your consent choice, and core platform operation.
- **Functional:** remembers preferences such as your theme and UI settings.
- **Analytics and performance:** enables full product analytics (PostHog) when opted in. Declining it does not turn off the cookieless counting described below, which stores nothing on your device.
- **Marketing and personalization:** optional category covering campaign and referral attribution (such as the `vr_referral` entry described below), and reserved for future marketing features.

### Cookies and Local Storage We Use

| Name | Type | Purpose | Storage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sb-<project>-auth-token` | Strictly necessary | Supabase authentication session | Cookie |
| `csrf` | Strictly necessary | Cross-site request forgery protection | Cookie |
| `consent_prefs` | Strictly necessary | Stores your cookie consent choices (up to 1 year) | Cookie |
| `theming` | Functional | Remembers your theme preference (up to 1 week) | Cookie |
| `sidebar_state` | Functional | Remembers whether the dashboard sidebar is expanded (up to 1 week) | Cookie |
| `vectreal-publisher-tab-id` | Strictly necessary | Distinguishes Publisher browser tabs so in-progress work is not mixed between them | Session storage |
| `vectreal-publisher` | Strictly necessary | Holds an in-progress upload locally until you save or discard it | IndexedDB |
| `ph_*` | Analytics | PostHog product analytics; set only after analytics consent | Cookie and local storage |
| `vr_referral` | Marketing | Remembers how you arrived (referrer, campaign source) for attribution; set only after marketing consent | Local storage |

Before you grant analytics consent, PostHog keeps nothing on your device: no cookies, no local storage and no session storage. Session replay, surveys and identification do not run, and events raised by our servers, including sign-in, sign-up, and contact form events, are not sent. If you are signed in, your user ID and name are sent to PostHog only after you grant analytics consent; being signed in is not by itself treated as consent.

### Counting Without Consent

If you decline analytics, or have not answered the cookie banner yet, PostHog runs in a cookieless mode and our site sends it only these events:

- a page view each time you open a page,
- viewing the newsroom listing (with the number of results shown and whether a search or category filter was used),
- viewing a newsroom article (its address, category and reading time),
- clicking the call to action at the end of an article (which article, where it leads, and which button), and
- loading a model into the Publisher (the file's format and how long loading took).

Each event carries only the page's address without its query string, any campaign parameters in that address (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`, `utm_term`), the website you came from (its domain only, never the page), your browser's user agent and the browser, operating system and device type derived from it, and the time. It carries no screen or window size, no language or timezone, no page title, no form input, no clicks elsewhere on the page, and no account or contact details.

To count visitors without storing an identifier on your device, PostHog's servers compute a one-way hash of your IP address, browser user agent and our domain together with a random value (a "salt") that changes every day and is deleted once that day's events are processed. The hash cannot be turned back into your IP address, and because the salt changes daily, the same visitor on two different days cannot be recognized as the same person. Your IP address is not stored and is not used to look up your location. This lets us see, for example, how many visitors read an article and then opened the sign-up page on the same day, but never who they were.

We rely on our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) in knowing how many people visit our site and whether our articles lead to sign-ups. Nothing is kept on your device for this purpose. You may object at any time by contacting us; because the data holds nothing that identifies you, we cannot single out your past entries (Art. 11 GDPR). Browser extensions that block PostHog also stop this counting. If you accept analytics, cookieless counting ends and full product analytics starts with a new identifier; what was counted before is not linked to it.

Referral and campaign attribution (`vr_referral`) is written only after you grant marketing consent, and is removed if you withdraw it.

Functional preferences (`theming`, `sidebar_state`) are written only after you grant functional consent. If you decline, those settings still work for the current session but are not remembered when you return.

### How Consent Works in the App

- On first visit, users are shown a consent banner.
- Users can choose Accept all, Reject all, or Manage preferences.
- Preferences can be updated later from the footer "Cookie Preferences" control or from dashboard settings.
- Consent choices are stored server-side and associated with either your account or an anonymous identifier, together with the policy version, derived country, and user agent, so we can demonstrate valid consent.
- If we materially change our consent categories, we increment the consent policy version (currently 1.0.0) and ask you again.

## 5. Legal Bases for Processing

Where the GDPR applies, we rely on one or more of the following:

- **Contractual necessity** (Art. 6(1)(b)): to provide the platform, accounts, and billing you request.
- **Legitimate interests** (Art. 6(1)(f)): for security, reliability, abuse prevention, and product operations, and for counting visits without cookies as described in Section 4, balanced against your rights.
- **Consent** (Art. 6(1)(a)): for optional categories such as analytics and marketing. You can withdraw consent at any time through the in-app consent controls, without affecting prior lawful processing.
- **Legal obligations** (Art. 6(1)(c)): for example, tax and accounting requirements.

## 7. Data Retention

We retain data for as long as necessary to provide the service, comply with legal obligations, resolve disputes, and enforce agreements.
