A 3D model looks like it's standing somewhere once it has a shadow. In the [Vectreal Publisher](https://vectreal.com/publisher?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-model-shadows-and-lighting) that takes a lighting setup that suits the product and one switch, Ground shadow, for a floor that catches the shadow its lights cast.

## Why does my 3D model look like it's floating?

Because nothing tells your eye where the ground is. Anything you'd put somewhere in real life, a car, a bike, a cup, a chair, needs a ground under it, and on a web page, unless the model brings its own floor, that ground is its shadow. Without one it hangs in front of the page like a sticker.

Not every model needs to sit on something, so the floor that catches its shadow starts switched off in the Publisher. When yours does, grounding it should be as easy as clicking a toggle, so it's one switch.

## One switch for the shadow

![Two views of the same plain grey stool. On the left it stands on an unlit floor with no shadow. On the right, a light low on the left lights the floor and throws the stool's shadow across it.](/media/images/newsroom/14/cast-shadow.webp)

*Left: no shadow. Right: the key light's shadow on the floor.*

The shadow comes from the lights. Every lighting setup places up to three studio lights, a key, a fill and a rim, on the bright spots of its environment, and the key light casts the shadow the model stands on. It has the model's real shape and falls the way a shadow does, instead of being an outline of the model feathered onto the floor.

Open the **Lighting** tool and switch on **Ground shadow**, and a floor appears under the model to catch it. That's what makes the model look like it's standing there rather than hovering.

To move the shadow, use **Turn**. It turns the lighting around the model, and the shadow swings with it. Under **Lights**, each light has its own **Strength**, **Softness**, **Direction** and **Height**, and the **Dramatic** setup gives the crispest shadow.

## Which lighting setup should I pick?

![Two views of the same stool. On the left it is lit by the Studio soft environment; on the right by Outdoor golden hour, and its seat and legs turn warm.](/media/images/newsroom/14/environment-light.webp)

*Left: Studio soft. Right: Outdoor golden hour.*

A lighting setup sets the light and the shadow together. The Lighting tool has eight: **Soft studio**, **Product**, **Dramatic**, **Overhead**, **Daylight**, **Golden hour**, **Overcast** and **Night**. A new scene starts on Soft studio.

Each setup is built on an environment map, a panoramic image of a scene that lights the model and shows up in its reflections. I went through a lot of them and picked a few per category, so you can match the light to the mood of the product. The setup places its studio lights on the map's own bright spots, so a highlight on something glossy sits where the reflection's light is. To try another environment, open **Environment** in the Lighting tool:

- **Studio:** soft, natural, key
- **Outdoor:** golden hour, noon, overcast, sky
- **Nature:** park, park overcast, snow, moonlit
- **Night:** city, building, stars, pure sky

![The same camera under five environments, each shown as the background: Studio soft, Outdoor golden hour, Nature park overcast, Nature snow and Night city. Each lens reflects a different scene.](/media/images/newsroom/14/lighting-presets.webp)

*Top: Studio soft, Outdoor golden hour. Bottom: Nature park overcast, Nature snow, Night city.*

**Brightness** makes any environment dimmer or brighter, and **Resolution** switches between 1k and 4k: 4k sharpens reflections on glossy models and takes longer to download. On anything glossy, paint, chrome or glass, the setup changes the reflections as much as the light, so click through a few on your own model before you settle.

## A background instead of a void

![Two views of the same stool under golden-hour light. On the left there is no background and it stands on a dark stage; on the right the blurred golden-hour scene is shown behind it.](/media/images/newsroom/14/background.webp)

*Left: no background. Right: the environment as the background, blurred.*

Show the environment behind it. In the **Background** tool, next to Lighting, pick **HDRI** and set **Blur** to Soft or Blurred, and the model gets a soft backdrop. **Color** and **Gradient** give a plain one instead. With the shadows on as well, the model reads as standing on a surface with a space around it, not floating in a void. It helps the shadows too: on a dark stage there's little for them to show against.

## Visitors see the shadow straight away

When you save, the Publisher stores the finished shadow with the scene, so whoever opens it sees the shadow from the first frame instead of watching it fade in.

## Do I need to know 3D to set this up?

No. I spent a lot of time regrouping the Publisher's controls so they aren't one long wall of sliders: light and shadow now live in one tool, Lighting, with the background in its own tool next to it. I want anyone and everyone to be able to play around with it and get nice results, whether they know 3D or not.
