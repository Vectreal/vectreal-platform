# Lighting

The **Lighting** tool in the Publisher sets how a scene is lit: the environment
it reflects, up to three studio lights, and the ground their shadows land on.
There are no shadow settings of their own. Every shadow follows from the
lights, so moving a light moves its shadow, and a softer light casts a softer
one.

---

## Setups

A setup is a whole look in one click: an environment, the lights placed on
that environment's own bright spots, and the balance between them. Each
thumbnail shows a chrome ball and a grey ball lit by it.

| Setup | Looks like |
|---|---|
| **Soft studio** | Even, wrapping light with a gentle shadow. The default. |
| **Product** | A smaller key with a fill and a rim, for crisp highlights and a defined shadow |
| **Dramatic** | A small, strong key and a rim over a dim environment |
| **Overhead** | A key from almost straight above, the shadow pooled under the model |
| **Daylight** | An outdoor sky with a small sun as the key, for a sharp shadow |
| **Golden hour** | A low, warm sun with a cool fill from the other side |
| **Overcast** | A soft sky and no added lights, for an almost shadowless look |
| **Night** | A dim city with a cool key and a warm rim |

Picking a setup keeps the background, the environment's resolution and the
ambient occlusion you chose. Change any light, the environment or the ground
afterwards and the label above the grid reads **Custom**. Pick the setup again
to go back to it.

**Turn** rotates the environment and every light together, so the reflections,
the shading and the shadow stay in agreement. Turning does not count as an
edit: the setup stays selected.

---

## Environment

The environment is a panoramic image that lights the model and shows in its
reflections. There are fifteen, grouped as **Studio**, **Outdoor**, **Nature**
and **Night**. Picking one keeps the lights where they are.

- **Brightness** scales the environment, and every light with it, because a
  light's strength is measured against the environment.
- **Resolution** is 1k or 4k. 4k sharpens reflections on glossy models and takes
  longer to download.

If a map cannot be loaded, the scene is lit by a neutral room instead and the
panel says so.

---

## Lights

A scene has up to three lights, one per role:

- **Key light**, the main light, which shapes the model and casts the shadow it
  stands on.
- **Fill light**, which softens the shadows the key leaves on the model.
- **Rim light**, which lights the model from behind to lift its edge off the
  background.

Open a light to change it:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **On** | Turns the light off without losing where it was |
| **Strength** | 1 is as bright as the whole environment |
| **Softness** | The light's size, in degrees as seen from the model. Larger is a softer light and a softer shadow |
| **Direction** | Degrees around the model, before **Turn** is added |
| **Height** | Degrees above the horizon; 90 is straight overhead |
| **Color** | A color temperature from 2000 K (warm) to 10000 K (cool); 6600 K is neutral white |

While the Lighting tool is open, the scene shows a handle for the light you
have open, or for the key light. Drag it to aim the light.

A shadow's darkness follows from the lights alone. Ground that a light cannot
reach keeps `1 / (1 + strength × sin(height))` of the light it would get, so an
overhead light at strength 1 leaves its shadow at half the brightness around
it.

---

## Ground shadow

**Ground shadow** puts a floor under the model that catches the shadows its
lights cast. The floor itself is invisible: only the shadow shows, over
whatever is behind the model.

---

## Background

| Background | Behind the model |
|---|---|
| **None** | Nothing. An embed shows the page it sits on. |
| **Color** | A flat stage in one color |
| **Gradient** | A stage shading from a top color to a bottom color |
| **HDRI** | The environment image itself, with **Blur** and **Brightness** |

The environment lights the model whichever background you choose. With
**HDRI**, the image has no floor, so a ground shadow has nothing to land on.

---

## Ambient occlusion

**Ambient occlusion** darkens creases and tight gaps on the model. It is drawn
every frame while the view moves, so it is off by default. **Only when still**
draws it once the view stops, and **Strength** sets how dark it gets.

It can also be baked: raytraced into the model's own textures when the scene
is published, so it costs nothing to draw and any glTF viewer shows it. Set
`lighting.occlusion.mode` to `baked` through the
[scene settings API](/docs/agents/api); the panel then reads **Baked**, and
switching the toggle on goes back to drawing it live.
[Publishing](/docs/guides/publish-embed#publishing-a-scene) says what baking
needs and what it skips.

---

## Scenes saved before the Lighting tool

A scene saved with the earlier Environment and Shadows tools is lit from those
settings, translated into an environment and, if it cast a shadow, a key light, and opens in the
Lighting tool that way. Saving it stores the lighting. Embeds running an older
`@vctrl/viewer` keep working: the server derives the older settings from the
lighting for them.

---

## From code

The scene settings API reads and changes lighting as the `lighting` section:
`GET` and `PATCH /api/v1/scenes/{sceneId}/settings`, or `updateSceneSettings`
in [`@vctrl/sdk`](/docs/packages/sdk). The section sent replaces the stored one
whole. Once a scene has a `lighting` section it supersedes `environment` and
`shadows`, which describe only a scene saved before it, so change `lighting`. The [`@vctrl/core`](/docs/packages/core) package exports the setups as
`LIGHTING_SETUPS`.
