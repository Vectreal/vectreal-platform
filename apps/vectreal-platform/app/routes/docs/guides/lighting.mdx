# Lighting

The **Lighting** tool in the Publisher sets how a scene is lit: the environment
it reflects, up to three studio lights, and the ground their shadows land on.
There are no shadow settings of their own. Every shadow follows from the
lights, so moving a light moves its shadow, and a softer light casts a softer
one.

---

## Setups

A setup is a whole look in one click: an environment, the lights placed on
that environment's own bright spots, and the balance between them. Each
thumbnail shows a chrome ball and a grey ball lit by it.

| Setup | Looks like |
|---|---|
| **Soft studio** | Even, wrapping light with a gentle shadow. The default. |
| **Product** | A smaller key with a fill and a rim, for crisp highlights and a defined shadow |
| **Dramatic** | A small, strong key and a rim over a dim environment |
| **Overhead** | A key from almost straight above, the shadow pooled under the model |
| **Daylight** | An outdoor sky with a small sun as the key, for a sharp shadow |
| **Golden hour** | A low, warm sun with a cool fill from the other side |
| **Overcast** | A soft sky and no added lights, for an almost shadowless look |
| **Night** | A dim city with a cool key and a warm rim |

Picking a setup keeps the background, the environment's resolution and the
ambient occlusion you chose. Change any light, the environment or the ground
afterwards and the label above the grid reads **Custom**. Pick the setup again
to go back to it.

**Turn** is the globe under the setups: the sky around the model, seen from its
front, with the horizon, the environment's bright regions and a dot for each
light in its color. Drag it sideways to rotate the environment and every light
together, so the reflections, the shading and the shadow stay in agreement.
Arrow keys turn it a degree at a time and Page Up and Page Down ten (Shift
multiplies either by ten), and a double-click turns it back to the front. It turns about the vertical axis
only: tilting would lift the environment's horizon off the ground. Turning
does not count as an edit: the setup stays selected.

---

## Environment

The environment is a panoramic image that lights the model and shows in its
reflections. There are fifteen, grouped as **Studio**, **Outdoor**, **Nature**
and **Night**. Picking one keeps the lights where they are.

- **Brightness** scales the environment, and every light with it, because a
  light's strength is measured against the environment.
- **Resolution** is 1k or 4k. 4k sharpens reflections on glossy models and takes
  longer to download.

If a map cannot be loaded, the scene is lit by a neutral room instead and the
panel says so.

---

## Lights

A scene has up to three lights, one per role:

- **Key light**, the main light, which shapes the model and casts the shadow it
  stands on.
- **Fill light**, which softens the shadows the key leaves on the model.
- **Rim light**, which lights the model from behind to lift its edge off the
  background.

Open a light to change it:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **On** | Turns the light off without losing where it was |
| **Strength** | 1 is as bright as the whole environment |
| **Softness** | The light's size, in degrees as seen from the model. Larger is a softer light and a softer shadow |
| **Direction** | Degrees around the model, before **Turn** is added |
| **Height** | Degrees above the horizon; 90 is straight overhead |
| **Color** | A color temperature from 2000 K (warm) to 10000 K (cool); 6600 K is neutral white |

A shadow's darkness follows from the lights alone. Ground that a light cannot
reach keeps `1 / (1 + strength × sin(height))` of the light it would get, so an
overhead light at strength 1 leaves its shadow at half the brightness around
it.

---

## Ground shadow

**Ground shadow** puts a floor under the model that catches the shadows its
lights cast. The floor itself is invisible: only the shadow shows, over
whatever is behind the model.

---

## Background

| Background | Behind the model |
|---|---|
| **None** | Nothing. An embed shows the page it sits on. |
| **Color** | A flat stage in one color |
| **Gradient** | A stage shading from a top color to a bottom color |
| **HDRI** | The environment image itself, with **Blur** and **Brightness** |

The environment lights the model whichever background you choose. With
**HDRI**, the image has no floor, so a ground shadow has nothing to land on.

---

## Ambient occlusion

**Ambient occlusion** darkens creases and tight gaps on the model. It is off by
default, and has two ways to be on:

- **Live** draws it every frame while the view moves. **Only when still** draws
  it once the view stops, and **Strength** sets how dark it gets.
- **Baked** raytraces it into the model's own textures when the scene is
  published, so it costs nothing to draw and any glTF viewer shows it.

With **Baked** chosen, **Preview the bake** opens [Bake](#bake), where you can
see it on the model before you publish.

---

## Bake

**Bake** writes lighting into the model when the scene is published, so the
viewer draws it rather than working it out. Choose what to bake:

| Setting | What it bakes |
|---|---|
| **Ambient occlusion** | Opens the Ambient occlusion view, where **Baked** writes the darkening in creases into the model's own textures. Any glTF viewer shows it. |
| **Studio lights** | The light and soft shadows the lights cast on the model, written into a light map. The Vectreal viewer draws the map in place of the lights' live light and shadows. Their highlights stay live. |

Baked studio lights look best on matte products, where the soft shadows a
model casts on itself are what you see. On shiny surfaces most of what you see
is reflection, which stays live either way. A few limits:

- The light map is stored in a Vectreal extension, `VECTREAL_lightmap`. Other
  glTF viewers ignore it and show the model with the environment and ambient
  occlusion but without the studio lights. So does an older `@vctrl/viewer`.
- The map holds the lights as they were when you published. If you change a
  light or **Turn** afterwards, the viewer draws the lights live until you
  publish again. The environment's brightness, and the map itself, scale baked
  light just as they scale live light.
- The bake follows the model's shape, not its normal maps, so fine surface
  detail does not shade baked light.
- A material that cannot be baked keeps live light. On top of the reasons
  ambient occlusion lists, that includes a material drawn from both sides,
  such as a label or a leaf: one map cannot light its front and back. So does
  a part an animation turns, moves or reshapes, because the map holds only
  the light of its rest pose. And while any part moves, or is drawn more than
  once, the whole model keeps live light: the map would hold that part's
  shadow where it stood, not where it goes. The same goes while a cutout
  material (alpha mask) casts a shadow whose mask the bake cannot read, since
  it would draw its leaves as solid cards: a KTX2 texture, one that does not
  decode, or any past the first 64 masks it can read. An unlit
  material has no light to bake.
- The lights still cast live shadows, which now shade only their highlights,
  so a baked setup costs about as much to draw as a live one.

**Preview the bake** runs the same bakes publishing will, in the background, and
shows the result on the model while the Lighting tool is open. **Show the bake**
switches between the baked model and the model as it is, to compare. The panel
then says how long it took and, for each bake, how many materials took it, and
lists each material that could not with the reason. If one bake fails, the
preview shows the other and says why, as publishing ships it. Changing what is baked, or
a baked light, discards the preview, as does a new optimization pass or model.
Publishing bakes again either way.
[Publishing](/docs/guides/publish-embed#publishing-a-scene) says what baking
needs.

## Scenes saved before the Lighting tool

A scene saved with the earlier Environment and Shadows tools is lit from those
settings, translated into an environment and, if it cast a shadow, a key light, and opens in the
Lighting tool that way. Saving it stores the lighting. Embeds running an older
`@vctrl/viewer` keep working: the server derives the older settings from the
lighting for them.

## From code

`GET` and `PATCH /api/v1/scenes/{sceneId}/settings`, or `updateSceneSettings`
in [`@vctrl/sdk`](/docs/packages/sdk). The section sent replaces the stored one
whole. Once a scene has a `lighting` section it supersedes `environment` and
`shadows`, which describe only a scene saved before it, so change `lighting`. The [`@vctrl/core`](/docs/packages/core) package exports the setups as
`LIGHTING_SETUPS`.
