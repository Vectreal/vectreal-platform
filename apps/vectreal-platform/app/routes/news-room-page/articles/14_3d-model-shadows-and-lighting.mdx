A 3D model looks like it's standing somewhere once it has a shadow. In the [Vectreal Publisher](https://vectreal.com/publisher?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-model-shadows-and-lighting) that takes two switches, one for a shadow cast by a light and one for a darker shadow where the model meets the floor, and then a lighting preset that suits the product.

## Why does my 3D model look like it's floating?

Because nothing tells your eye where the ground is. Anything you'd put somewhere in real life, a car, a bike, a cup, a chair, needs a ground under it, and on a web page, unless the model brings its own floor, that ground is its shadow. Without one it hangs in front of the page like a sticker.

Not every model needs to sit on something, so shadows start switched off in the Publisher. When yours does, grounding it should be as easy as clicking a toggle, so each shadow in the Publisher is one switch.

## The cast shadow and the ground shadow

![Two views of the same plain grey stool. On the left, with Cast shadows off, it stands on an unlit floor. On the right, with it on, a light low on the left lights the floor and throws the stool's shadow across it.](/media/images/newsroom/14/cast-shadow.webp)

*Left: Cast shadows off. Right: on.*

**The cast shadow.** Switch on **Cast shadows** and the Publisher computes a shadow from a light above the model. It has the model's real shape and falls the way a shadow does, instead of being an outline of the model feathered onto the floor. Three presets, Soft, Studio and Dramatic, set its darkness, softness and angle together, and you can drag the light in the scene to change where it falls.

![Two views of the same stool with its cast shadow. On the right, with Ground shadow on, the floor is darker right around its feet, where they touch it.](/media/images/newsroom/14/ground-shadow.webp)

*Left: Ground shadow off. Right: on.*

**The ground shadow.** With Cast shadows on, switch on **Ground shadow** for a smaller, darker shadow right where the model touches the floor. It's what makes the contact convincing: the wheels of a car or the base of a cup look pressed against the ground rather than hovering just above it. It starts at values that work, and you can soften or spread it if you want.

With both on, the model looks like it's standing on the floor.

## Which lighting preset should I pick?

![Two views of the same stool. On the left it is lit by the Studio soft preset; on the right by Outdoor golden hour, and its seat and legs turn warm.](/media/images/newsroom/14/environment-light.webp)

*Left: Studio soft. Right: Outdoor golden hour.*

The light on the model comes from an environment map, a panoramic image of a scene that lights the model and shows up in its reflections. I went through a lot of them and picked a few per category, so you can match the light to the mood of the product. They're under **Environment**, in the **HDR preset** menu:

- **Studio:** soft, natural, key
- **Outdoor:** golden hour, noon, overcast, sky
- **Nature:** park, park overcast, snow, moonlit
- **Night:** city, building, stars, pure sky

![The same camera under five lighting presets with the background shown: Studio soft, Outdoor golden hour, Nature park overcast, Nature snow and Night city. Each lens reflects a different scene.](/media/images/newsroom/14/lighting-presets.webp)

*Top: Studio soft, Outdoor golden hour. Bottom: Nature park overcast, Nature snow, Night city.*

A new scene starts on Studio natural, and the **Lighting** slider makes any preset dimmer or brighter. On anything glossy, paint, chrome or glass, the preset changes the reflections as much as the light, so click through a few on your own model before you settle.

## A background instead of a void

![Two views of the same stool under golden-hour light. On the left the background is hidden and it stands on a dark stage; on the right the blurred golden-hour scene is shown behind it.](/media/images/newsroom/14/background.webp)

*Left: Background hidden. Right: shown and blurred.*

Show the environment behind it. Under **Background** in the Environment section, switch on **Show** and turn **Blur** up to Soft or Blurred, and the model gets a soft backdrop. With the shadows on as well, the model reads as standing on a surface with a space around it, not floating in a void. It helps the shadows too: on a dark stage there's little for them to show against.

## Visitors see the shadow straight away

When you save, the Publisher stores the finished shadow with the scene, so whoever opens it sees the shadow from the first frame instead of watching it fade in.

## Do I need to know 3D to set this up?

<video src="/media/images/newsroom/14/shadows-panel.mp4" poster="/media/images/newsroom/14/shadows-panel-poster.webp" autoPlay muted loop playsInline aria-label="A vintage camera in the Publisher. No. I spent a lot of time regrouping the Publisher's controls so they aren't one long wall of sliders: shadows have their own section, and lighting and background sit together under Environment. I want anyone and everyone to be able to play around with it and get nice results, whether they know 3D or not.

Before you publish, check the result the way a visitor will see it in [preview mode](https://vectreal.com/news-room/preview-a-scene-the-way-it-will-be-embedded?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-model-shadows-and-lighting).

## Try it

Drop a GLB, FBX or STL file into the [Publisher](https://vectreal.com/publisher?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-model-shadows-and-lighting), or the folder that holds a glTF or OBJ. Switch on Cast shadows and Ground shadow, and click through the lighting presets. You can try all of it without an account; you sign in when you want to save. If the model is headed for a product page next, [how to add a 3D model to a website](https://vectreal.com/news-room/how-to-add-3d-model-to-website?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-model-shadows-and-lighting) covers the rest.
