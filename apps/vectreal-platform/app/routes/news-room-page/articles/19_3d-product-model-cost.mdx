## What does each part cost, at a glance?

Published prices, checked on 29 October 2026. Every figure is the seller's own unless the row says otherwise.

| What | From, as published |
| --- | --- |
| A studio model of one simple product | $42 to $179 |
| A studio model of a complex product | $300 and up |
| A freelance entry package | $15 to $50 |
| A scanning app for your phone | Free to $150 a year |
| An AI-generated draft | About $0.20 to $0.50 in subscription credits |
| A CGI render on a white background | $5 to $20 per image, plus the model |
| A studio packshot photo | €9 to $39 per image |
| Showing the model on Shopify or Amazon | No charge |
| A hosted viewer or a Shopify 3D app | $15 to $949 a month |

The sections below give the sources and the conditions, which matter more than the numbers in several cases.

## How much does a studio charge to model one product?

Most studios don't publish prices and quote per catalogue. Vizbl says so outright: ["We don't publish per-model prices because every catalog is different."](https://dev.vizbl.com/3d-modeling-services) In Shopify's Partner Directory, one of the sixteen partners on the first page of its [3D models category](https://www.shopify.com/partners/directory/services/visual-content-and-branding/create-3d-models-ar) shows a price. The rest say "Contact for pricing".

These are the studios that do publish one, for a web-ready GLB:

| Studio | Simple product | Complex product | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Modelry](https://www.modelry.ai/pricing) (formerly CGTrader Arsenal) | From $42 on its pricing page, from $60 in its [FAQ](https://www.modelry.ai/faq-category/plans-pricing) | Quoted | The two pages disagree |
| [CGI Furniture](https://cgifurniture.com/service/glb-3d-model-services/) | $60 | $300, ultra complex from $375 | For orders of 200 or more models a year; smaller orders are quoted |
| [UFO3D](https://ufo3d.com/3d-ecommerce/) | From about $80 | "A typical product runs about 200 US dollars" | Two rounds of revisions included |
| [Sayduck](https://www.sayduck.com/pricing) | From $179 | Quoted | One round of revisions included |

Shopify's own blog gives a wider range: $40 to $200 for a simple product and more than $1,000 for a complex one. That [post](https://www.shopify.com/ae/blog/3d-model-products) dates from 2020 and doesn't say where the range comes from.

## What do freelancers charge?

Less, and with more spread. On Fiverr, five gigs offering GLB product models had entry packages of $15, $15, $15, $35 and $50. That's a handful of listings rather than a survey, each covers one simple product at its cheapest tier, and every seller notes that the price moves with complexity. [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/3d-artists/) says its freelance 3D artists typically charge $25 to $40 an hour.

## What makes one model cost more than another?

The studios that publish prices also say what moves them:

- **Complexity.** UFO3D measures it in time: one to two days for a bottle or a box, three to five for furniture, small appliances or shoes, one to two weeks for electronics, machinery or jewellery. CGI Furniture counts parts, materials, curved or soft shapes and moving mechanisms.
- **Variants.** A product in six colours is a base model plus work for each option, each with its own materials.
- **Your reference.** CAD data gives exact dimensions and speeds the work up, and photos without clear measurements lead to more revision rounds, per UFO3D. [How to get a 3D model of your product](https://vectreal.com/news-room/how-to-get-a-3d-model-of-your-product?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-product-model-cost) covers what to hand over.
- **Volume.** CGI Furniture's list prices assume 200 models a year, and UFO3D says the cost per product falls for families that share materials and setup.
- **Revisions.** Sayduck includes one round and charges for more. UFO3D includes two.

## Is scanning it yourself cheaper?

Often, yes, if the product scans well. Shopify's app has a [3D scanner](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/3d-scanner) for iPhone 12 Pro and later and recent iPad Pros; Shopify's [blog](https://www.shopify.com/in/blog/3d-ecommerce) says it comes at no additional cost. Amazon announced a free scanning tool in its Seller app for brand-registered US sellers in 2024, and [Amazon](https://sell.amazon.com/tools/3d-ar) charges nothing for 3D models on listings. Of the standalone apps, [Polycam](https://poly.cam/pricing) has a free tier and a Basic plan at $150 a year or $30 a month, and [KIRI Engine](https://www.kiriengine.app/pricing) Pro is $79.99 a year.

The catch is the same as with any scan. Shopify's own help page lists smooth, single-colour, reflective, soft and very thin products as hard to scan well, so check your range against that list before you plan around it.

Scanning services for e-commerce products, such as VRModelFactory and Frame Sixty, quote per job. The published scanning prices are industrial ones, like [prtwd](https://prtwd.com/services/3d-scanning/)'s "Scanning projects start at $250", for files meant for engineering rather than a product page.

## Are CGI renders cheaper than a photo shoot?

Per image they can be, once the model exists.

| | Per image | What else |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CGI render, white background | From $5 (Full HD) or $7 (4K) at [CGI Furniture](https://cgifurniture.com/service/silo-3d-visualization), at 200 or more items a year; from about $20 at UFO3D | The model, priced separately |
| CGI lifestyle scene | $100 to $290 at CGI Furniture; from $120 at UFO3D | The model, priced separately |
| Studio packshot | $39 at [soona](https://soona.co/pricing), plus a $149 studio pass or a membership | |
| | €49 at [Welpix](https://welpix.com/product-photography-prices/), minimum project €999 | |
| | €9 at [Marszal Studio](https://marszalstudio.pl/en/product-photography-pricing/), net of VAT | |

Squareshot, which listed product shots from $50 an image, announced on its homepage that it would close on 31 October 2026.

The model is the expensive part, and it's paid once. After that, CGI Furniture prices extra colour variants of a render from $0.20 each, and the same file can go on the product page as something visitors turn around themselves. With photos, every new angle is another shot to book.

## What does an AI-generated model cost?

Very little per model, if you count subscription credits:

- **[Meshy](https://www.meshy.ai/pricing) Pro** is $20 a month for 1,000 credits, and an image-to-3D generation costs [20 credits](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing). That's up to 100 models, about $0.20 each.
- **[Rodin](https://hyper3d.ai/pricing) Creator** is $30 a month for "about 60 models", about $0.50 each.
- **[Tripo](https://tripo3d.ai/pricing) Professional** is $19.90 a month, or $15.90 billed yearly, for 3,000 credits. Its pricing page doesn't say how many credits a model takes.

Those per-model figures assume every generation is usable and leave out the time spent fixing what isn't. Free tiers exist, but Meshy's free models are licensed CC BY 4.0, which means crediting Meshy, and Tripo's free models are public. Whether a generated model is accurate enough for a product page is a bigger question than its price; [the AI section of this guide](https://vectreal.com/news-room/how-to-get-a-3d-model-of-your-product?utm_source=newsroom&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=3d-product-model-cost) goes through what gets guessed.

## What does it cost to show the model on a page?

On the big commerce platforms, nothing extra:

- **[Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-media-types)** takes GLB or USDZ files up to 500 MB as product media, and neither the help page nor the plan comparison mentions a charge. The theme has to support 3D, which Shopify's own Online Store 2.0 themes do.
- **Amazon** says: "There is no cost for adding 3D models and augmented reality to your Amazon product listings."
- **[Google Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13675100?hl=en)** shows 3D from a GLB you host yourself, states no fee, and limits it to home goods and shoes sold in the US, Canada, Australia, Japan and India.

On your own site, you pay for a hosted viewer or you host the file yourself:

| Option | Published price | Worth knowing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Sketchfab](https://help.sketchfab.com/en/articles/16152171-pro-membership) Pro | $15 a month billed yearly, $24 monthly | Embedding is allowed on every plan, but removing Sketchfab's branding takes [Premium](https://help.sketchfab.com/en/articles/16152181-premium-membership), at $79 a month yearly or $129 monthly |
| [SwiftXR](https://apps.shopify.com/swiftxr-viewer) (Shopify app) | $25 to $200 a month | Plus $1 per 1,000 views over the plan; the free plan carries a watermark |
| [LEVAR](https://apps.shopify.com/levar-final) (Shopify app) | $59 to $249 a month | 20 to 100 models |
| [MAZINGXR](https://apps.shopify.com/mazing-app) (Shopify app) | $99 to $949 a month | 10 to 500 models |
| [Zakeke](https://admin.zakeke.com/en-US/pricing) | From $69.90 a month | Plus 1.5% to 1.9% of each customised product sold |
| Threekit, VNTANA | Not published | Both quote per customer |
| Google's [model-viewer](https://npmjs.com/package/@google/model-viewer) | No licence fee | Open source (Apache-2.0); a web component, and you host the files |
| [`@vctrl/viewer`](https://github.com/Vectreal/vectreal-platform), which I work on | No licence fee | Open source (AGPL-3.0, a copyleft licence); a React component, and you host the files |

Watch the per-view and per-sale fees. A flat monthly price is easy to compare; $1 per thousand views or a share of every sale grows with exactly the traffic you added 3D to get.

## Does 3D pay for itself?

The evidence is thinner than the figures that get quoted. The most careful study is a peer-reviewed field study of a cosmetics retailer's app, published in the [Journal of Marketing in 2022](https://www.ama.org/2021/04/20/augmented-reality-in-retail-and-its-impact-on-sales-2/): 160,400 customers and more than 800,000 sessions. AR use went with higher sales mainly for niche and more expensive products and for customers new to the app or category, and the authors write that ["the overall impact appears to be small."](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6696)

A vendor-run A/B test is the next best thing. 3D supplier Fibbl showed the 3D viewer first instead of photos on 274 GANT products across 13 markets and reports a [6.3% uplift in conversions](https://fibbl.com/gant-3d-first-conversion-case-study/), without saying what the baseline was.

The often-quoted 94% increase in conversions comes from a 2020 Shopify blog post that cites no study. The figures for fewer returns that circulate alongside it don't link to a primary source either.

Read together, the evidence says 3D helps most where photos leave a shopper unsure, on unusual, expensive or unfamiliar products. That's also where the model is worth paying for first.
